### Why the simulation attains approximately 0.58×

The raw arithmetic is decisive about the clock input, separately from the stall source:

```text
Independent wall duration                 60.162876100 s
Sum of supplied Godot _Process delta       35.074055333 s
Difference                                25.088820767 s
35.074055333 × 80                          2805.924426667 requested ticks
Minus final fractional debt                   0.924426667
Equals                                    2805 completed ticks
2805 / (60.162876100 × 80)                 0.582792949×
```

The application's 16-tick frame cap is **never reached**: maximum scheduled/advanced ticks is 12, maximum residual debt is 0.999467, zero frames report overload. The application therefore completes the ticks requested by its engine-supplied delta; the missing wall time is already absent before `FoundationClock.Schedule` receives it. The maximum observed delta is 150 ms, despite much longer wall intervals.

The pinned local `GodotSharp.xml` documentation for `Node._Process` explicitly warns that accumulated delta can differ from real time due to engine capping and delta smoothing. Runtime reports TimeScale=1, MaxPhysicsStepsPerFrame=8 and PhysicsTicksPerSecond=60. The documentation's simple ratio gives 133.333 ms, whereas these samples reach 150 ms; no exact internal cap/jitter-compensation derivation is claimed without an engine trace. The **measured delta-sum accounting**, not that nominal formula, establishes the throughput mechanism. Changing the clock input would be a separate proposed correction and would not remove the underlying rendered gaps; no such change was made.
