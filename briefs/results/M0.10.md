# M0.10 — Crowd benchmark and foundation gate

Implementation status: **Measured; M0 performance gate failed**. Independent acceptance review is pending. This result does not mark M0.10 accepted.

## Measurement contract

`CrowdBenchmarkFixture` runs eight isolated production `GameSession` instances as service destinations, staggered at controller ticks 0–560. They use production physical-arrival queues, deterministic varied walking speeds, pathfinding/separation, stock, wallets, transactions and exits. This is not one shared occupancy world: agents in different sessions cannot collide or compete for the same space, so the fixture cannot establish whole-festival crowd capacity.

A comparable headless sample begins at tick 600, after all eight sessions and every declared agent are active, and measures 300 ticks. Reports record active sessions/agents at both window boundaries. The runner is deliberately unpaced: its capacity is `1000 / (80 × mean tick ms)`, relative to the production clock's 80 ticks/real-second at game 1×. The requested-speed label does not schedule wall time and is not attained-speed evidence. Representative requested/attained 1× and 4× rendered behaviour remains **Unverified**.

The narrow/wide fixtures share demand, stock, starts, service slots, exits and opening centre; only opening width changes (3 versus 17 cells). Wider access also permits some agents a shorter route, so the deterministic completion difference supports only this fixture, not a general or statistical congestion claim.

The 1,200 diagnostic uses 4 warm-up plus 12 pre-arrival ticks after normal warm-up proved structurally impractical. It is launch/initialization evidence only. Completion may continue after representative measurement for functional reconciliation; completion timing is not mixed into the measured window.

## Rendered evidence

- Exported 50 and 500 captures each measured only 25 frames through controller tick 30, when one of eight sessions was active and attendees were still pre-arrival. Their frame percentiles are launch diagnostics, not representative population measurements.
- Exported 1,200 initialization was bounded at 217 seconds / 215.4 CPU seconds, became non-responsive and produced no frame. This fails the 1,200 rendered target; it does not provide a frame percentile.
- Therefore representative rendered FPS, the under-8-ms-per-rendered-frame ceiling target, and requested/attained 1×/4× rendered speeds are **Unverified** below 1,200. No early-frame number is used as a pass.

## Gate interpretation and follow-up

- **50** is the only currently evidenced representative headless scale with substantial compute margin, and the accepted M0.09 exported foundation separately completed 50 attendees at an observed 3.997× phase speed.
- **200 is not a reliable 1× baseline**: its unpaced upper bound is only 0.245–0.270× game 1× on this machine.
- **500** remains a deterministic functional stress fixture, not a real-time capacity claim.
- **1,200** remains an eventual perceived-crowd ambition; full-fidelity M0 support failed.
- A separately approved optimization brief should investigate shared route/corridor caching, staggered planning budgets, destination flow fields and simulation LOD/aggregation, while preserving authoritative money, occupancy and visible-agent causality. No structural rewrite was attempted here.

## Limitations

- Eight isolated sessions are not a shared world, and only the first session is presented in rendered mode.
- Representative rendered population, GPU timing, allocation rate and draw-call counts are unmeasured.
- The valid process-peak measurement covers new 50/200 runs. The old 500/1,200 working-set samples were not true process peaks and cannot pass the ceiling memory gate.
- Timings are one machine/run per configuration, not statistical distributions across repeated runs or hardware.
