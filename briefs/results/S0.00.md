# S0.00 — population architecture diagnostic

Implementation status: **Accepted diagnostic; targeted architecture spike recommended.** The representative 200 run is functionally correct but has poor arrival mean/tails. The congested 200 run reached all timed windows but timed out during bounded completion, leaving an explicit partial checkpoint. Acceptance confirms that the implementation and evidence truthfully answer S0.00; it is not a supported-capacity claim. No production optimization, clock/fidelity change or R0.01 gameplay was performed.

## Fixture and measurement contract

`ScaleDiagnosticFixture` creates one production `GameSession`, one traversal/occupancy world, 50, 100 or 200 distinct navigation agents and wallets, and three physical service queues with independent stock, queue membership and exit ownership. The representative layout uses the inherited Lower Wittering farm terrain and spread destinations. The congested layout adds a real north/south fence with a two-cell opening and compact east-side holding pens, forcing entrance traffic through one corridor. Six (four at population 50) deterministic fixture-only pre-admission retargets exercise ownership transfer; they are not gameplay AI.

Each run measures 120-tick arrival, service-congestion and **early-transaction** windows. The latter begins after at least three purchases; it exercises non-empty transaction/history hashing but does not measure materially grown late history. Every timed tick verified the full navigation and wallet counts, globally unique queue-agent ownership, and no failed/completed queue lifecycle. The runner then continues within 25,000 ticks at 50/100 or 60,000 ticks at 200 to verify final reconciliation. Save/restore hash identity is checked after the transaction window.

The opt-in `ScaleDiagnosticProbe` is deliberately non-authoritative and absent from hashing/persistence. Its categories are nested but reported non-overlapping: route search; movement/separation excluding navigation-owned searches; queue work excluding queue-owned searches; snapshot construction excluding hash; hash; and signed unaccounted runner/tick work. The pre-window validity snapshot now occurs before timing/allocation baselines. Negative exclusive/reconciliation values beyond a 1%/0.25 ms tolerance abort the run rather than being clamped. Allocation and GC counts cover each complete timed window. The measured total includes the normal snapshot returned by every production `AdvanceTicks(1)` call.

## Bottleneck breakdown

The data supports two measured walls rather than the earlier unmeasured assumption that all crowd work scales alike.

- **Avoidance-owned global routing causes the 100-person arrival tail.** Representative arrival performed only 19 searches, all avoidance-triggered, but expanded 23,765 nodes and consumed 43.5–45.0% of measured time; congested arrival performed 27/27 avoidance searches, expanded 42,514 nodes and consumed 57.6%. These rare full-grid replans explain the low medians alongside 28–34 ms p99 ticks. At 200, representative/congested arrival expanded 75,355/102,147 nodes. This is measured route time, not movement time.
- **Snapshot/hash is the steady per-tick floor.** Hash alone consumed 59.1–74.2% of measured 50-person windows and 24.4–62.8% at 100, except when route spikes dominated. This is normal publication performed by `AdvanceTicks`, not a separately forced diagnostic snapshot.
- **Allocations are material and correlate with snapshot/search work.** Window allocations were 70.9–77.2 MiB at 50; at 100 they were 77.1–128.6 MiB representative and 94.0–159.4 MiB congested. The 200 arrival windows allocated 303.1 MiB representative and 344.1 MiB congested. The probe does not attribute individual allocations, so the source split is inferred from cost/counter correlation, not directly measured allocation call stacks.
- **Queue work itself is not the primary wall in this fixture.** Exclusive queue time peaked at 13.60 ms total across a 120-tick window (0.113 ms/tick average). Queue compaction does trigger route work: for example, 100 congested early-transaction state recorded 446 searches, 420 avoidance-triggered and 9,828 expanded nodes. Those search costs are reported under route search rather than double-counted as queue time.
- **Blocking is layout-sensitive.** Maximum cumulative blocked age at window end reached 155 ticks in 100 representative and 109 in 100 congested; representative 200 reached 431 and partial congested 200 reached 757. All completed runs still finished without route failure, backlog or leaked ownership. The timed-out congested 200 checkpoint cannot establish final correctness.

## Correctness

Every retained 50/100 run and the 200 representative run passed save/restore hash identity and completed all declared purchases with one wallet/person, correct cash and stock, zero route failures, zero final queue backlog and all slot/exit reservations released. Representative 200 completed at tick 40,481. The congested 200 checkpoint is deliberately partial and makes no final reconciliation claim. Final hashes exist only for completed JSON reports. Same-seed/layout authoritative state is deterministic; the two representative 100 seeds deliberately produce different hashes, while their path-search counts/nodes are identical because fixture geometry does not consume randomness.

## Recommendation and next proof point

Choose option **(a): a measured targeted architecture spike**, not a broad replacement or product population reduction yet.

1. Separate bounded local collision/yield recovery from global A* routing so an avoidance offset does not synchronously repeat a full-grid search. Preserve deterministic stable-ID ordering, exact physical positions, queue ownership and identical off-camera logic; identities, tickets/wallets and save state remain one-per-person. Any planning budget must be deterministic and persisted if it spans ticks.
2. Change snapshot publication so the scheduler does not construct and SHA-256-hash a complete public snapshot after every individual authoritative tick. Keep authoritative state hashing available at explicit verification/save boundaries, and preserve exact save/restore/replay equivalence. This changes publication cadence, not simulation causality or the 80-tick clock.
3. Add allocation attribution for pathfinding versus snapshot construction before choosing data structures. Do not infer an allocation fix solely from the combined window counter.

The next proof point after the targeted spike is **100 first**, using both layouts/two seeds with correctness/save identity, mean and p95 below 6 ms and p99 below 12.5 ms, with no blocked-age regression. Only then repeat bounded 200 representative and congested runs. Rendered/input-pacing work should follow only if headless 200 meets those declared criteria. This spike preserves the product model; no evidence requires aggregation, wallet removal, camera-dependent causality or a lower clock.

## Limitations

- Timings are one machine and 120-tick windows, not a hardware distribution or long soak.
- The second seed repeats representative 100 only; congested 100 has one timing run. Seed changes authoritative identity/hash but not fixture geometry, so identical route counts are expected.
- The probe adds stopwatch/counter overhead; no uninstrumented paired timing was used. It does not affect authoritative hashes, as covered by a focused test.
- GC generation counts are process counters over the window, while bytes are current-thread allocations; neither is an allocation call-stack profile.
- The early-transaction window has only 3–4 transactions at its start; materially grown history cost is unmeasured.
- Strict rendered 60 FPS, frame/input pacing and OS input latency were not measured because headless 200 already missed the declared mean/tail targets.
- Congested 200 completion is partial due to the exact-child 120-second timeout. 500 and 1,200 were not run. Final supported capacity remains unproven.
