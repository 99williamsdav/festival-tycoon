## Acceptance limits and review

- The user provisionally approved Playtest 8 audio: the set-start cheer is quiet but suitable for this excitement level; band-entry applause is somewhat loud for the current crowd and fine calibration is deferred. The three cheer/applause recordings are CC0 and provenance-recorded. This is not final production-audio approval; ordinary crowd ambience is still absent. Automated checks cannot approve aesthetics.
- The fresh revised-scene VSync-on/58 exported runs meet the provisional speed and p95 frame thresholds in both tiers. Isolated 100–256 ms hitches remain, Tier 2 narrowly missed the fixture's maintenance-completion condition at 60 seconds, and one run per tier does not prove stable performance or resolve the earlier VSync tail's cause.
- A fresh independent Astra-low review found one non-simulation presentation bug: loading a pre-show save from a live show stopped audio but left the old live cue and stage lights visible. Reset now clears both labels, cue colour and lights; a targeted independent re-review accepted the repair with no remaining findings. The reviewer independently reran the 30 focused tests before the repair; the post-repair Godot C# build passed with zero warnings, and a separate Playtest 9 Windows export passed a headless startup smoke test. No rendered save/load reproduction was run after the repair.
- Clickable identity/attendance inspection and saved records are implemented; visual click targeting was smoke-tested through scene startup, not manually exercised by the user. Full human playtest pacing and input latency remain unverified.
- Focused transition/persistence/code review found and repaired retry-route churn, delayed-boo timing, stage side access and continuous deck elevation, post-exit `OnStage` attribution, one-second enjoyment overcredit, stale load visuals, and performer cleanup on terminal failure. The playtest correction then relocated the authoritative trailer and sound/lights/cues together, constrained nav to the GLB's visible stair footprint, moved the audience into the front apron, made stronger fans more crowd-tolerant, enlarged the default window/labels and advanced/softened the boo. The follow-ups gave every walking human the same presentation-facing behaviour, modestly widened the ordinary camera and moved the generator outside the apron without weakening the hazard or repair window. The latest follow-up added the user logo to engine boot/start screens and moved the band to a timed off-stage hold with mark-time instrument attachment and a fixed drum prop. The full unrelated suite was not rerun after focused fixes; the parked TrafficProof failure is not an R0.02a regression claim.

R0.02a is accepted within the 25/45-person prototype boundary. Do not interpret this as stable-60-FPS, 200-person, full-attempt pacing or final production-audio acceptance. R0.03 remains a separate start decision with its own asset and timing gates.
