# R0.02a — live performance and audience

Status: **Implemented — verification pending.** Approved band/assets and temporary music are integrated; focused and rendered functional checks pass. Current default rendered throughput and human audio audition are not accepted; see [result](../results/R0.02a.md). Inserted between accepted R0.02 and medical R0.03 so the festival's central show is playable before adding more incident chains.

## Stakeholder outcome

One booked three-person band actually reaches the existing trailer stage, plays one fixed short set, and draws a visibly non-uniform audience. Guests autonomously choose places according to their enthusiasm, sightline, local crowding and travel; enjoyment changes from time genuinely spent watching. The player hears an outdoor stage performance and audience reactions while moving the camera. No attendee is directly controlled by the player.

## Bounded design

- Reuse the existing folk/punk booking choice, trailer stage, owned/rented sound rig and generator. One stage, one fixed approximately two-minute set after the audience has had time to arrive; no timetable editor, multi-stage clashes or additional booking economy in this slice.
- Represent the act as three named protected performers rather than one visual token; keep Tier 2's total active people at or below 50. Each performer retains one stable identity/body and physically travels to and from the stage via a valid side/entry route. No backstage building is required now; a future backstage area must not require a new person identity or a permanently instrument-bearing character. In the normal route they cannot be silently teleported on or off stage. **Approved playtest amendment:** the band first waits off-stage at reachable ground access, enters via the visible stairs shortly before the scheduled start, and starts only after all three physically reach their marks. Guitar and bass attach at those marks before the music starts; the drum kit remains a fixed stage prop before, during and after the set. No pickup/carry/backstage transition animation is needed.
- Show pre-set, live, interrupted and finished states. The performance clock and booking/attendance/enjoyment records are authoritative, deterministic, hashed and saved. Audio playback position, animation and camera mixing are presentation, not causes of simulation decisions.
- Replace the current one-time arrival music satisfaction award with bounded enjoyment from actual attendance duration, act/guest genre fit, show quality and powered sound. Avoid double reward across save/load. A guest who never reaches a listening area is not counted as a listener.
- Generate many walkable candidate places across front, middle and rear listening areas, not one destination or a perfect grid. Score a shortlist for each guest using enthusiasm/front preference, sightline, nearby occupied density, path/travel cost and a stable per-person offset. Strong fans claim available front places first; indifferent guests tend to watch from farther back; middle-interest guests favor a comfortable view. Occupancy, route safety, stage access and egress override preference: no guest may reserve an occupied/blocked place, shove through people, teleport, or gain an impossible guaranteed front position. Stable-ID tie breaks and infrequent event/cooldown-based reconsideration prevent visual shuffling and route churn. Off-camera logic is identical.
- Stage music uses two original or properly licensed, provenance-recorded folk/punk loops. It is positioned at the stage; a light outdoor PA reverb/colouration is mixed with distance attenuation and increasing high-frequency roll-off as the camera moves away. Avoid a cavernous indoor echo. Use a listener/camera-distance calibration appropriate to the high isometric camera so zoom does not make the show inaudible everywhere. Mute remains supported with a readable visual live-set cue.
- Crowd reactions are short, pooled audio/visual events driven by actual on-site listener count and aggregate enthusiasm plus the booked act's popularity/appeal. Cheers swell at meaningful set moments; they do not play per person or continuously at full volume. If power cuts, let the sudden silence register without an immediate gasp or confused-reaction sample; retain ordinary ambience where available, then use boos/bemusement only if the interruption persists, scaled by actual audience and expectations. Safe isolation remains immediate and valid; dissatisfaction is a bounded consequence, not a new death/disorder chain.
- Generator load shedding dims the show while preserving reduced sound; emergency isolation silences sound/lights until repaired or otherwise made safe. Exact show-quality response must be coherent with R0.02's existing equipment states and cannot undermine its safety counter. The presentation reacts to authoritative state; it does not own the incident clock.

## Asset and audio gate

Reuse the approved stage and attendee proportions/style. Before build, design one prototype band-member/instrument visual and obtain coordinator and user approval of that exact version; then proceed one item at a time to complete the three-person set and modest stage performance cues. The instrument must be a detachable accessory, not baked into the person's body. Show the same persistent character in neutral walking/idle without the instrument and performing with it attached; do not spend this slice on carrying or pickup animation. Preserve compatible scale, facing and palette/attachment conventions so clothing colours, hair and simple face variants can be added later without replacing identity or authoring a separate character for every act; full cosmetic customization is not required in this slice. The existing generic attendee is unrigged and one-material, so do not claim it already supports swappable hair/face or full walk/play animation. Sol is the preferred design model. The three band-member/instrument assets are now approved and committed. The original procedural folk/punk loops are approved as temporary prototype music. The CC0 HowardV boo is provisionally acceptable for sustained interruption; its gasp is reserved for a future witnessed death/shock cue. Placeholder sounds may be used for remaining gaps; the user will audition Soundly later. Record provenance and muted visual equivalents, and do not silently treat placeholders as final.

## Acceptance and stop

- Normal playable route shows booking → performer travel → set start → autonomous front/mid/rear audience → time-based enjoyment → set end. Inspect individual attendance/reason and actual positions; the audience is not placed in robotic rows or across exits.
- Controlled same-seed comparisons change music fit, crowd density/slot availability, route delay and equipment power. Strong-fan front preference holds when safe space exists; full frontage produces the best reachable alternative. Repeated reconsideration does not oscillate or multiply global route searches without bound.
- Save/reload during travel, set, interruption and completion preserves authoritative timing, ownership, attendance and enjoyment exactly, without replayed cheers as authoritative effects or duplicated satisfaction.
- Demonstrate camera-near/far audio level and muffling, light outdoor reverb, cheer scaling and prolonged-interruption reaction, including muted visual equivalents. Human audition approves aesthetic balance; automatic tests verify only state and measurable gain/cutoff behaviour.
- Remeasure the shared 20/40-guest scene with the three performers and equipment worker, report attained speed/frame tails, and do not infer 200-person capacity. Keep parked P3 traffic proof untouched. Stop before medical R0.03, timetable expansion or disorder work.
