# S0.03 — movement architecture review and proposed decision

Status: **Design review complete; implementation choices pending user approval.** This is not an implementation brief or a capacity claim. R0.01 remains paused. No authoritative code changed during this review.

## What the evidence establishes

- [S0.00](../results/S0.00.md) completed the representative 100- and 200-person headless fixtures, but 200 missed arrival timing targets and congested 200 timed out without final reconciliation. Avoidance-triggered full-grid route search causes tail spikes; snapshot construction and hashing are a steady cost.
- [S0.01](../results/S0.01.md) removed most avoidance search and improved timed windows, but two versions failed 100-person bounded completion. [S0.02](../results/S0.02.md) captured pre-admission corridor contention and tested two waypoint-ownership rules; they too failed bounded completion. Neither result proves permanent deadlock or rules out all cooperative traffic designs.
- The current implementation is a useful **completed-fixture reference**, not a general physical-motion proof: a separation sidestep may span 300/600 mm in a tick with about 30 mm nominal forward allowance, interpersonal clearance is checked at endpoints rather than over simultaneous swept paths, and a same-cell destination can be marked arrived without centring. These are code-contract gaps, not established causes of the spike failures.
- The S0.00 service fixture uses dispersed holding bays: approaches initially target different bays and queue compaction may reroute people across them. Preserve this geometry as a regression fixture; add a separate production-like single-file-lane fixture rather than silently replacing it.

## Proposed architecture

Use **two independent tracks**. First, optimize the completed-fixture baseline without changing its decisions: an indexed priority queue and reusable indexed arrays for the 256×256 A* grid, preserving exact tie order, selected path and expanded-node count; then separate authoritative stepping from snapshot/hash publication, with canonical capture at explicit save and verification boundaries. Each change is measured separately. This buys headroom but does not solve physical-motion or liveness contracts.

In parallel, design an explicit deterministic traffic kernel outside production navigation. A person remains an individual with fixed-point position, walking speed, identity, wallet, intent and route. A tick reads one start state, generates speed-bounded candidate movements, arbitrates contested swept space, then atomically commits positions, reservations and queue transitions. A denied move must not consume distance or route progress. Different camera/publication schedules must yield identical authoritative state.

Start with **controlled conflict regions** at narrow passages and service ingress/egress, not a global next-waypoint owner. A passage request has a stable fairness ticket, direction, entrance and secured exit berth. Entry is denied if there is no safe place to discharge; an occupant retains rights until physically clear. Opposite directions take turns in finite batches. Adjacent regions without safe holding space must be treated as one region. Open-space crossings use deterministic local conflict groups and simultaneous swept-path checks; moving into genuinely vacated space is permitted only if the joint movement is safe. Persistent cycles trigger a bounded recovery plan using reachable refuge positions, not repeated priority flips. If a bounded planner cannot find a solution, report an unresolved planning state; only call a layout infeasible when that is established under an explicit supported-layout rule.

The service queue keeps physical admission order, exact service-anchor arrival and the existing purchase ledger. Ingress ownership does not grant logical queue precedence. Slot compaction needs a real movement grant; a departing buyer still physically occupies space until clear. Cancellation, retargeting and closure release obsolete future claims atomically while preserving present occupancy and safe departure rights; save/restore must preserve that state.

## Contract and proof gates before production integration

1. Specify dimensions and semantics: configured person clearance, terrain/body clearance, tick speed cap, exact-arrival tolerance (prefer exact service anchor), supported queue/exit geometry, and what happens when a player layout is physically infeasible. Do not infer these from the current heuristic.
2. Define canonical state for active claims, intent/region versions, fairness tickets, progress ages and recovery. Persist, validate and hash every decision-affecting field. Derived spatial indices and route caches may be rebuilt only when that cannot alter decisions or timing of authoritative grants. Plan a versioned legacy-save path.
3. Build a small executable kernel proof before replacing production navigation: opposing passage traffic; crossing trajectories and swaps; slow leader and faster follower; blocked exit; continuous demand in both directions; circular wait with and without refuge; service ingress against egress; slot advance while a departing person still occupies space; cancellation/retarget; save→restore→continue during each contested mode. Assert speed and swept clearance every tick, fairness and eventual completion under explicitly feasible layouts. Count meaningful progress toward unchanged intent, region discharge and service—not just lateral motion or a reset blocked-age counter.
4. Differentially test A* path and expanded nodes, unchanged final hashes/completion ticks in both original 100-person layouts, and publication-frequency equivalence. Retain the original dispersed-bay fixture plus a new realistic lane fixture. Run 200 headless only after both 100 layouts and seeds complete correctly, then mature-history and rendered probes. Do not weaken population, 80-tick/second clock, per-person wallets, off-camera causality or the old fixture to obtain a pass.

## Proposed sequencing and stop rules

**P1 exact-result A* optimization**, then **P2 snapshot/publication separation**: independent briefs with baseline comparisons and rollback on any changed route, hash, completion or save continuation. These are performance work, not movement acceptance.

**P3 isolated traffic-kernel proof**, then **P4 controlled integration** of one passage and one service into the production simulation. Stop at the first unresolved safety or feasible-layout completion regression; one focused repair pass per stage, then escalate with the failing minimal case. Do not build R0.01 gameplay against unproven movement semantics.

Risks requiring evidence include automatic conflict-region extraction from player layouts, safe holding capacity, complex dispersed-bay compaction, recovery in dense cyclic crowds and planning cost when many people share a conflict group. A finite-horizon planner alone cannot guarantee completion. Any proposal to constrain placement, change queue admission, allow overlap, reduce clearance or cap population is a product decision, not an engineering default.

## Decisions requested before implementation

- Accept the two-track order: exact-behaviour performance work first, traffic-kernel proof in parallel or next, with R0.01 paused.
- Treat speed-bounded movement and swept person/terrain clearance as design requirements even where the current prototype violates them; retain the provisional 300 mm minimum **centre-to-centre** separation until a visual/scale test justifies a change. It is not a 300 mm body radius.
- Defer exact placement restrictions and player-facing behaviour until the kernel reveals what it supports. Any future rule may flag or reject a layout only when a documented supported-layout constraint is violated or infeasibility is established. An unresolved bounded planner is not proof that the geometry itself is impossible. No valid layout is guaranteed progress merely by a fairness counter.
