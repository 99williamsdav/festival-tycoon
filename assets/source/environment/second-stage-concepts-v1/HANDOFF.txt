CHOICES / PRACTICAL MODELLING NOTES
A — TIMBER DAIS
Low chamfered front, broad integrated step, stout exposed timber support frame.
Cleanest open silhouette and the simplest carpentry language. Distinct from the
previous taller rectangular rail-enclosed upgraded-stage concept: no perimeter
rail enclosure; front step and chamfered outline are defining features.
Model deck/slab, step, posts and repeated short braces as separate simple parts.
Use broad warm timber palette blocks; no need for every painted grain/nail.
Keep corner stubs low and stairs readable. Main sheet's performers are context.

B — FARMYARD PLATFORM (RECOMMENDED FOR FARM CHARACTER)
Low rectangular deck, sleeper perimeter, reclaimed vertical front fascia, squat
masonry piers, side stair and bunting fixed below deck level. It feels deliberately
assembled for this farm yet retains more handmade character than hired equipment.
Model a stable continuous floor above the frame; no pallet holes or loose bales.
Reuse plank strips and a few pier meshes. Bunting is one sparse simple strip;
keep it below performers. Rear remains open rather than a wall/backdrop.
This is the strongest rural fit while visibly moving beyond a wheeled trailer.

C — MODULAR RISER
Charcoal panel deck, exposed dull steel support grid, underdeck diagonals and
separate metal stair. Strongest visual contrast to the trailer and most clearly
purpose-made stage equipment, while still modest hire-kit rather than arena rig.
Repeat a small deck-panel/frame module and leg/foot unit. Keep braces under deck,
short guard sections at corners only and central rear open. Avoid increasing
rail height/length into a cage. Small bolts can be palette cues at game scale.
Generated sheet's descriptive 'affordable/easy to set up' wording expresses the
visual hire-kit idea; it is not an assigned game cost or setup-time mechanic.

SHARED CONSTRAINTS
Uncovered in every direction: no roof, awning, overhead crossbeam, tall backdrop,
large banner, lighting tower or new effects. No mosaic 3D treatment. Instruments,
microphone, speakers and people are removable context, not merged stage geometry.
Open low silhouettes are intended to preserve performance readability from all
four camera rotations. Rear/side illustrations were inspected; four native
camera visibility is NOT verified because no models exist yet. Precise deck
height, footprint, performer marks, access and operational clearance remain
implementation questions after selection, not specifications inferred from pixels.
Generated projections are visual studies, not orthographic construction drawings.
Same illustrated adult height and comparable deck widths on comparison board
support fair visual comparison; no exact dimensional equivalence is claimed.
No new crowd/interaction/placement rules or automatic upgrade progression.
